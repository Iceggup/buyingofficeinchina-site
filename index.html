What I do

Four things, in the order they happen

You keep the contract and you keep the money. I do the work in between, and put all of it in writing.

Find and verify the factory

Three to five workshops that actually make your product — not a directory listing. Business licence, production line, export record and references are checked before a factory is put in front of you.

Compare the quotations properly

Unit price, minimum order, lead time, payment terms, packing and port, set side by side. The lowest headline price is rarely the cheapest order, and you will see why before you commit.

Speak to both sides in their own language

Asking, clarifying, pushing back and revising — from Chinese on one side to English on the other, with each step written down and sent to you. Nothing rests on what someone remembers being said.

Inspect before shipment, and watch the loading

Quantity, specification, finish and packing checked against your order before the container is sealed — with photographs, a written report and the seal number recorded.

Why it helps

What a buyer gains from having someone on this side

Not a longer list of services — a different position. These are the four things that change when your eyes on the ground are local.

I understand how both sides think

A Chinese factory quotes the way it always has — on habit, on a relationship, on a handshake that is supposed to hold. A buyer wants every number fixed in writing before any money moves. Neither side is being unreasonable. The gap between them is where orders stall, and where money is quietly lost. I stand on both sides of it, so the gap closes in days instead of months.

A pair of eyes that lives here

Catalogues, screenshots and listings all look the same. A workshop floor does not. I see the machinery, the stock, the packing area and the people — and I form a view on whether a promise is likely to hold. That judgement cannot be made from ten thousand kilometres away, however many emails are exchanged.

A shorter road from enquiry to container

Questions that would cross time zones for a week get answered in a day. Quotations are chased, translated and compared in parallel instead of one at a time. Samples move, documents move, and the order starts sooner — which also means your money is tied up for less time.

I work for you — and only for you

The factory is not my client. You are. So when a price is low for a reason, when a specification quietly changes, or when a delivery date is being padded, my job is to tell you — not to smooth it over. That is what buyer-side means in practice, and it is the whole point of hiring someone who is not paid by the other side of the table.

Where I start

Wire mesh and fasteners

Both are made within two and a half hours of my door. That is why I start here — I can stand in these factories, read the documents and check the goods myself, instead of relying on a flight, a hotel and five days in the country.

Wire mesh — Anping

Anping makes more than 80% of China's woven and welded mesh: welded panels, chain link, hexagonal netting, stainless and galvanised rolls.

Checked every time: mesh aperture, wire diameter, material, weave or weld type, roll width and length, weight per roll, coating, and packing.

Fasteners — Yongnian

Yongnian produces around 55% of China's fasteners — bolts, nuts, washers, anchors and threaded rod, in the full range of DIN, ISO and ANSI standards.

Checked every time: size and thread, grade, material, surface finish, standard, head marking, packing — and the mill certificate read against the goods.

The two go together. An importer of construction hardware rarely buys one without the other, so a single requirement can run across both — one conversation, one glossary, one set of shipping and document arrangements.

These two are where I start, not where I stop. I live inside the North China industrial belt, so when your product is made by another cluster a few hours away, the same work applies. Send it, and I will tell you honestly whether I can cover it properly — before you spend anything.

How it works

Four steps from your specification to a sealed container

  1. 1

    You send the requirement

    Product, specification, quantity, packing, destination port, and the delivery window you are working to. Use the form, or send it on WhatsApp if that is quicker.

  2. 2

    We agree the scope and the fee, in writing

    I confirm exactly what I will deliver and what it costs, before any work starts. If you would rather your money sat with a third party, the first project can run through Upwork milestone escrow.

  3. 3

    I visit, verify and bring you the comparison

    Three to five factories screened and visited. Credentials checked, production capability confirmed, quotations collected and set side by side with the technical documents behind them.

  4. 4

    You contract the factory; I see it loaded

    You sign with the factory and you pay the factory directly. Before shipment I inspect the goods against your specification and supervise the container loading, with photographs and a written report.
